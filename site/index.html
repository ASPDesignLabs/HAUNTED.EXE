HAUNT.EXE

80×25 text, 720×400

A possessed 486 PC. It boots, a ghost types back at the DOS prompt, and the programs have been got at: EDIT rewriting AUTOEXEC.BAT for a séance, DEFRAG moving souls to the front of the disk, a modem call to a BBS on the other side, MSD finding GHOST.SYS on IRQ 13. Between them come seven half-block ANSI scenes, from the jack-o'-lantern to a ghost that rushes down a hallway. It ends in a fatal error and a CRT power-off, then reboots.

CATACOMB

Mode 13h, 320×200, 256 colours

A Catacomb 3-D style crawl through crypts generated for each level: halls two and three cells wide, big chambers and colonnades, hung with chains, hooks, gibbet cages and chandeliers. Forty named levels come in four runs of ten, from the Crypts down to the Deep, each a place of its own in its own stone, rotting further towards its tenth, where a boss waits in an arena of its own. The caster shops at the merchant, a cat on a treasure chest, for spells and things to wear that are rolled like loot, and takes its runes from a shrine across the room, for Cursed Coins. She also sells a map that fills in as you walk, and turns what you have slain and found into Levels of Atonement, points to spend on the caster. Then it fights twenty kinds of creature with nine attack spells and nine runes, opening colour-coded vaults and hidden walls on the way to a rune-sealed exit. Some of those walls open on chains of places that shouldn't be under a crypt at all: the Backrooms, and Severance. Will-o'-wisps fly ahead to the way out; Black Altars send their own to the secrets. Each run has hazards of its own in its walls and rooms; on its even levels a sealed wing opens only to a puzzle worked out in the world, and from the third level a door marked with skull and bones holds a trial against an hourglass. Play it yourself and death costs: only the merchant's ledger keeps what you have, and her hall, with the bosses' gifts in it, is what you keep when you fall.

Four places

Each run of ten is built in its own stone, with its own rooms, doors, keys, light and treasure, and its own way of rotting from its first level to its tenth. The walls are laid as a mason would lay them: a stretch at a time, by the room each faces, and where one material gives way to another the cell between is a blend, blocks falling away or a skin fraying, never a hard edge. Floors and ceilings come from the room. The merchant's hall is rebuilt once in the Crypts' ashlar, the same before every run; the pockets keep their own looks.

I. The Crypts levels 1 to 10

The burial vaults of a forgotten order: dressed ashlar, coursed rubble in pale lime, lime plaster with a painted dado, the bedrock, and bone walls in the ossuaries only. Burial vaults with niches in rows, chapter houses with banners, tablets and a lozenge floor, knights' vaults with their slabs, undercrofts cut from the rock. Torches in iron sconces, oak doors under iron, vaults strapped in their key's metal with the Order's cross, keys with a trefoil bow. Kept at first, then damp, then falling in. The knight fights in a knights' vault.

II. The Catacombs levels 11 to 20

Tunnels cut into wet slate beneath them, flooded and drained: bedded slate, slate lining, silt and river stones, flowstone, and the dead the floods brought down. Saints' catacombs, cisterns with standing water, silted naves, canals and spinners' galleries. Lanterns on hooks, candles at the saints' feet, sluice doors gone to rust, corroded keys on a ring, coin gone black in the silt, reliquaries and pearls. A flood line climbs the walls as the run goes on, and webs come onto them from its sixth level. The water is walked through as any floor, but every step on it splashes. The spider's gallery is a spinners' gallery, its bile coming out of culverts.

III. The Hollows levels 21 to 30

Caverns and an abandoned mine where the dead were tipped: rough granite, the pick-cut face, timber shoring, basalt columns, and backfill with the dead in it. Timbered galleries with rails and carts, stopes, charnel pits, fossil halls, and a cult's chapel at the end. Oil lamps on spikes, rough mine doors and chained vaults, heavy keys with a tally tag, nuggets, raw gems and a strongbox. Worked, then abandoned: scree banks up the walls, boards split and fall, and late in the run something has dragged its claws down the stone. The Deacon keeps his rites in the chapel.

IV. The Deep levels 31 to 40

Black glass at the bottom: obsidian, basalt, ash-crusted rock, amethyst, and the dead fused into the glass. Geodes, glass halls, cinder fields, magma vents and a throne room. The walls are the light: magma fissures in the torches' place, geodes alight. Black iron doors with the fire behind their grilles, keys of glass, coin run molten, glowing crystal and an idol of black glass. It glitters on its first levels, burns through its middle, runs molten, and goes dark at the end, which suits the Wraith, whose arena is the throne.

What you wear seven places, five qualities

Seven places: a bracer on each wrist, boots, an amulet, a hood, robes and a ring. Every piece is rolled, and its quality shows in its colour: Common in white, Uncommon in green, Rare in blue, Ultra-Rare in purple, Exquisite in gold. The better the quality, the more stats and the stronger: armour, dodge, health, health restored, wards against poison, burning and slowing, movement, quiet footsteps, spell haste, spirit cost, damage, fire, frost or lightning, and spell effect; and since 1.0.0b11 an element's Power, Efficiency or Insight, a third of them leaning to the lines you feed, Resist Spirit, and Vessel, which carries more souls. A hood always carries an ability, other pieces sometimes: Swift, Arcane, Treasure Hunter (a whisper near secret walls), Keymaker (keys shine through the stone), Communing (creatures as red shapes through the walls), Thorned and Mirrored. About one piece in ten comes Anointed, a little stronger. The robes colour your sleeve, the bracer shows as a cuff, the ring's stone takes its colour and the hood frames your face.

The merchant two pedestals to seven

Each level begins in her room. A fresh run has two pedestals; more are bought from her menu, up to seven, and kept. She sells no spells now: every pedestal holds a thing to wear, and very rarely the first holds the BFS9000. One purchase a visit in the first run, two in the second, three from the third. A piece is worn the moment you buy it. You start without a bag; bags hold two to five spare pieces, and without one, what a new piece replaces is lost, so the card warns you first in red. With a full bag you are asked which spare to let go. Q holds a ware for your next visit. Runes aren't sold there.

The Rune Shrine for Cursed Coins

Across the room from the wares it stands pale and sanctified, and gives nothing until a Cursed Coin curses it. Then E opens its nine slots: pull with one to three coins and runes come into them, the more coins the more runes; three alike become one of the next quality; raise its rank with coins for better ones, or favour a kind you know. Each pull earns one rune to take, and takes not used are lost when you leave the room. Runes come in the same five qualities as what you wear, one of each held, the best, and at Exquisite each does a second thing: the Ward bursts into shards, Blink leaves a double that creatures go for, the Veil sends missiles home twice as hard, and so on. Coins come at the end of each chain of secrets, and one more for finding every secret on a level.

The Alignment and what you were six lines, twelve ultimates, six classes

Spells are lines now, one for each of six elements: Fire, Frost, Lightning, Poison (the Blight Flask, lobbed and splashing), Spirit (Soul Drain) and Physical (the Bone Spear). Each Level of Atonement brings an Alignment Point, spent at the Lampstand in a new room north of hers: three open a line, every point after is a rank, and a spell's strength follows your Level of Atonement and its rank. Each line runs through Efficiency and Insight to an ability, then down one of two paths to an ultimate: Flamewalker or Firewall, Winter's Lance or Shatter, Arc or Amperage, Plague Pit or Contagion, Soul Tether or Wisp Host, the Phalanx or the Ballista. Hold a spell to charge its ultimate, with a gathering by your hand in its element's colour. Every spell winds up before it goes, and a blow can break it.

At a fresh start she asks what were you? The Witchfinder, the Anatomist, the Galvanist, the Plague Doctor, the Sin-Eater or the Headsman: each a past with a story, its line opened free and its gifts. From Level of Atonement 20 she will hear another past, for five Cursed Coins. Pieces to wear roll the elements too.

Souls the vessel, the bell, the font

Your souls are your spirit, and spells spend them by rank; with too little a spell goes out weak. A vessel holds them, twenty and two a Level of Atonement, more with the Sin-Eater and what you wear, and what comes in over it slips away. The dead leave their souls where they fall, a pale flame for six seconds: walk through it. She takes ten for experience. In the new room the Dead Bell, cursed with a coin, takes souls to corrupt the next level: more of the dead, more gold, half the experience, and on a level you have cleared before, its secrets shown from the start. The Weeping Font, cursed with a coin, takes souls once a visit for a tenth more experience on the next level. The Accursed Portal replays any level you have cleared, for souls. Bats from rank II latch on to your face and feed on your health and souls until you shake them off, and spectres, now on every level by your Luck, can be slain by Spirit for their souls. Every Bone Spear throws a bone, which skeletons, shield-bearers, knights and hounds drop now and then.

Secrets and chains the Backrooms, Severance

Every level hides three to six secrets, more towards the end of each run. A secret wall looks like plain stone until you are a step or two away, when cracks show in it. Press E and it grinds back through a room carved out of the rock. Inside is treasure and often a potion, never a key, and none of it counts towards the level's treasure, so whatever you find is extra.

Some secret rooms have a doorway at the back. Walk into it and you are somewhere else, and the level waits for you, its clock and its creatures with it. In the first two runs it is the Backrooms: yellow wallpaper, damp carpet, tube lights humming, some failing, some dead, things from the crypt hung from the ceiling or sunk in the floor, the level's own creatures glitching, and no music, only the hum. In the next two it is Severance: white halls, green carpet, offices of four desks, every panel lit, creatures lit too brightly, and a muzak that is slightly too calm. Each link deeper is bigger and its creatures a rank higher. At the end a visportal, or a water cooler, restores you once, with the chain's Cursed Coin. Die in a chain and you come back whole at the secret's entrance, and that chain's doorway is sealed for the rest of the level: the dweller failed this trial.

Wisps and the Black Altars

Press L and a wisp to the way out costs a soul. It kindles before you and flies off down the halls the way you would walk, fetching the key first if the exit is in a locked vault, and waits at the doorstep. It lights the dark as it goes, shows the shades and drives the Wraith back, and leaves motes of light hanging for twelve seconds, so you can follow it round corners or after a fight. With a Keymaker piece on, there is a wisp for each key still lying in the level, in that key's colour.

From level 6, an altar stands deep in each level that has secrets. Press E at it for its three gifts, each with its price and a note on why it can't be had yet. Ten spirit wakes its wisp: a violet flame lights in its bowl, and a violet wisp leads you, free, to the nearest secret wall still shut, through any vault key on the way, and waits there while you are inside. Once you are out, E at it sends it home; from the altar you send it on to the next, for a little more each time. Twenty-five blesses your exit wisps until you leave the level: they wear a pale gold halo, and the undead near them flee. Find every secret and the flame burns gold; then it anoints one thing you wear that isn't anointed already.

Who lives down there twenty kinds, five ranks

About one new kind of creature a level over the first two runs, each a level or two after its sign, and alone the first time. In the Crypts, skeletons, bats and spectres, shamblers climbing out of turned graves, skulls in the walls, shield-bearers, spiders in their webs, the Drowned rising from puddles, bone hounds in pairs, and from level 9 the Wraith, once a level, which nothing kills: fire and a wisp's light only drive it back. In the Catacombs, hunting spiders on the ceiling, ghouls that feed on the dead, skeleton knights whose plate comes off as you hurt them, brood mothers with their egg sacs, shades you see only in light, which take your souls, ember imps by the magma, deacons that raise the dead (kill them first), and gargoyles that are statues until you pass. The Hollows and the Deep have all of them, higher in rank.

Each creature comes in its own colour or in green, blue, red or black, each tougher, quicker and worth more souls than the last, and the mix climbs over each run. Now and then one is gilded: it runs when hurt, and carries treasure.

Achievements and the bestiary

Each run keeps a record of what it has killed, the secrets it has found, the levels it has cleared and the bosses it has slain, kept on its page in the ledger and never lost to death. Achievements come for 60 of a creature's lowest rank and fewer for each rank up, for secrets from the first to the fiftieth, for each of the forty levels, for each boss slain and slain again at each step of its challenge, and for each time down after; they slide in at the upper right. Press B for the bestiary: a page for each creature, turning on the spot in each rank you have met, with your kills and what hurts it, learnt from three kills with each spell. The bosses have no pages yet. What the autopilot earns in an ordinary screensaver session isn't kept.

Hazards, puzzles and trials

Each run keeps things of its own that hurt, a sealed wing on its even levels that only thought will open, and from the third level a door marked with skull and bones. None of them is a menu: everything is done with E and your feet, and read in the world. And going out with anything left behind, the dead tell you what.

Each run's hazards six or seven a run

Some are fixed in the walls and rooms and keep a rhythm that can be learnt: a censer on its chain, a burial slab breathing, a sluice gate, a lodestone seam, a fissure, a black mirror. Some arise near you: votive racks falling, silt, choking ash, glass that spikes. There are more of both as the run goes on, and now and then one of an earlier run's. Each harms by an element, never more than a quarter of your health at a stroke, and always warns first. From the sixth level of each run, two or three on a level drink your souls while you stand in them: a reliquary, a weeping saint, a tithe box, the mirror. Resist Lightning is a new stat, rolled where the other resistances roll, with a line of its own in the points.

Puzzles twenty-seven kinds

On the even levels from the sixth, but never a boss's, a great door seals a wing with a hoard inside, and on one puzzle level in three the way on. No spell opens it. Skulls carried to their niches, bells rung in order, knights' heads turned, a scale balanced, cisterns poured, stepping stones that hold or sink, rails switched, a fossil's bones fitted, prisms turned into the magma's beam, a glass floor, singing crystals, a magic square: each run has its own, and two belong to any run. You carry one thing at a time and can still cast. After three wrong tries a whisper gives a nudge.

The answer, written down and the Black Shrine

The answer is on the level, hidden in one of the run's things: a candle-box, a satchel in the silt, a miner's boot, a broken geode. E searches one, a second's work, and the right one glints when you are close, more so with Treasure Hunter. A Black Shrine on each puzzle level will, for souls, show where it lies through the walls and on the map for a while. What you find, and every clue you read, goes into a new page of TAB, documents, until the level ends.

Trials seventeen, a minute each

On every level from the third, a door marked with the run's skull and crossed bones and two red lights. A step inside and it seals, the room turns about you (its walls, its light, a quicker march), and an hourglass stands at the top of the view with a minute of sand and no numbers. In the Crypts the ossuary's bones stand up in waves, a vigil is kept over three candles, relics are carried to a bier, a knight lays a wager and small bells answer only under the great one's toll; in the Catacombs the water rises, a brood ripens, sluice wheels are turned and saints' lamps lit in the dark; in the Hollows a roof comes down, ghouls feast, four deacons chant and firedamp fills the room; in the Deep the magma rises, braziers gutter through the dark hours, a glass court is raised again by its crowned one, and the glass sings.

Won, the hourglass breaks, you are made whole (spirit to half the vessel), and the trial's boon gives a fifth more experience from what you slay for the rest of the level. Let the sand run out and its creatures crumble, and the trial's curse takes a fifth from everything instead. Falling there is a death like any other.

The reckoning before you leave

Going out by the exit with anything still on the level (creatures, secrets, the sealed wing, gold, the trial), a slip pinned to its door says what, under the dead keep count, and you choose to descend below. It starts on not yet, so a quick E never leaves by mistake. The record counts puzzles solved and trials won, with achievements for them, and the Codex has a page for each hazard met, each kind of puzzle solved and each trial won.

Four bosses

The tenth level of each run is an ordinary level with an arena set into its far end in place of the exit: an antechamber with a candle and a bench, an arch, and the boss's own ground beyond. Step through and the arch seals behind you, the boss's card shows with a line of lore, its waltz begins, and its health runs along the top of the view. At 75, 50, 25 and 10% it stops, however hard the blow, turns untouchable in a pale shell and calls a wave of adds, at the highest rank of each kind you have met since you last died; when they are down it rises and fights on. Three fonts in the walls each hold a third of your health. When it falls it drops its Named Odious Scrip, the exit opens, and the run is complete.

The Ill-Fated Skeleton Knight level 10

He rode down to fetch the order's dead, and never rode back up. Polished plate with a glint sliding across it, shed at the first and third waves, quicker each time. Swift Dodge carries it two to five cells in half a second, untouchable: to close on you, to get away when hurt, or aside from a shot. Its Mirror Rune turns about one missile in seven back at you, with a silver ring and a struck bell. It plants its sword and the ceiling comes down round you, and raises it for the Sundering Arc, a pale quarter-circle on the floor before it that it sweeps a moment later.

The Wandering Hunting Spider level 20

It has walked these cisterns since before the order came. All of them are its. Up to the ceiling every few seconds, out of reach, across it and down near you with a leap. Its Mend Rune knits it back together, glowing green. It spits a web that holds you until your spells have torn a third of your health out of it (fire tears twice as fast), fires egg sacs onto the walls that hatch four small spiders, and opens outlets in the cistern's floor that spill bile, which poisons and slows. One small spider more comes out of a burrow every fifteen seconds.

The Black Deacon level 30

Black robes at twice a deacon's size, his Bone Ward wheeling about him as six shards; it takes the blows first, and grows back. He chants skeletons up from the chapel's heaps of bone, one at a time, unless a tenth of his health in damage breaks the chant. His Litany is two seconds of green light, then a fifth of your health if he can see you: the timber props hide you, the pews don't. Cracks run across the floor, glowing, until fire bursts from them.

The Wraith Unbound level 40

Eight braziers light its hall, and it gutters any it passes. It can be hurt only near one that burns, or near a wisp; in the dark your spells pass through it. E kindles a brazier again, a wisp at once. Time Slip slows a missile about to strike it, and it steps aside; Wraithwalk takes it into the stone and out of a wall beside you. Rings of void open where you stand and become purple columns: inside one you are slowed, sapped and silenced, with no spells, runes or abilities. Away from the light, hands reach up out of the floor.

Hazards and omens

Every hazard warns before it harms, never for less than about a second: grit and a hardening shadow before a rock, cracks glowing before the fire, a sheen creeping out before the bile turns foul, a ring and a thin column before the void. They harm only you, never the boss or its adds. On the last three levels before a boss, and in its own level's halls, its hazard comes into the crypt now and then as an omen, near you and at half the harm, more often the nearer you get: the ceiling drops a stone, bile spills from a grate, the floor splits and burns, the void opens.

The waltzes

Each boss has a slow waltz in a minor key over a five-note figure that drifts against the bar, a bell on the first beat: the knight's in D minor on brass, the spider's in B-flat minor, plucked, with a skittering hi-hat, the Deacon's in G minor on an organ with a choir, the Wraith's in A minor, a drone with a voice that comes and goes. While the adds are out it turns up, with drums, a doubled bass and a semitone higher, and when the boss falls it fades under three tolls.

Challenges steps one to five

A beaten boss can be fought again from the Accursed Portal, as you are, from its antechamber. Each win takes it a step higher, to five: more health, harder blows, its hazards and casts more often and more of them, its adds a rank or two up; from step 3 a win gives two scrip.

Death costs

While it plays itself, the screensaver dreams: it plays on from your last page, keeps nothing, and when it dies the level begins again, a little softer each time. Hold H and the run is yours until the program closes, sleepwalking or not, and death costs. The gold you carry, everything you wear, the bag, every spell but the Fire Bolt (back to the fireball), your souls, bones and Cursed Coins, your Levels of Atonement and what they bought all go; a stone is cut in the grave plot with what you carried and what took you, and you wake in the merchant's room before level 1. The runes, the Rune Shrine, the map and the merchant's hall stay.

The ledger three pages, signed by her

The merchant keeps three pages, and only she writes them. Press E at the cat and sign the ledger: the run as it stands goes onto a page, the one you came from picked first, and a page signed in her room keeps her visit too, the same wares set out when it is opened again. A page is written by a signature, a death and her claim, and by nothing else: fleeing keeps only what was signed, and the next start opens your last page. Open the ledger from ESC to load a page anywhere.

Her claim the dead are watching

While you are in danger (a creature hunting you close by, a missile on its way, health under a quarter, poison, a web, a hazard, a boss's arena) your page is marked, and the mark clears eight seconds after the danger has passed. A red eye shows under your keys while it holds. Open a marked page, by the ledger or the next start, and she takes the dead's claim, whatever you meant by it: a quarter to a half of your gold, and one piece you wear or a tier of one spell. Then she signs the page herself. A closed window, a crash or a power cut counts the same.

The grave plot

South of her room, the last ten deaths, a stone each, the newest freshly cut with its earth turned. E at a stone opens its page: the epitaph, the date, and what was carried, each worn piece with its card.

The merchant's hall

Her room has grown. Past an arch beside her is a back room, past another the grave plot, and nothing in any of it is lost to death. Press E at the cat for her menu: sign the ledger, ascend, buy another pedestal (500 gold for the third, up to 19,530 for the seventh), offer a coin for Second Wind, pat her, or leave.

The Hexed Totem the bosses' gifts

Each boss that falls teaches its ability here, raised to rank III with its scrip; scrip to spare sells for 20,000 gold. Swift Dodge, the knight's: tap a direction twice and dart two to five cells, untouchable. Tangled Web, the spider's: a glob of web holds a creature and sets it clawing at the web instead of you. Grave Call, the Deacon's: what you slew lately gets up again on your side for a while. Wraithwalk, the Wraith's: for a moment the creatures lose you and you walk through them. The right mouse uses the one readied, Q readies the next, and their sockets sit at the bottom left.

The Accursed Portal runs and challenges

A red-violet swirl in the grave plot's far wall. Each run whose boss you have beaten on the way down opens its start here, forward or back, as you are; below them, each boss slain, to challenge again at its step.

The Corpus Compendium

A tome on a lectern that writes down every piece you wear, as you first wear it, and every spell at its highest tier. Its pages sell them back at the shop's price and a fifth, a little less for each run completed, outside the visit's wares.

The Wretched Repository

Iron doors in the back room's wall. Pieces go in for a tenth of their price, gold less a quarter, Cursed Coins free; everything comes out free but the coins, at 2,500 gold each. Two places at first, twelve at most, and neither death nor her claim touches what is inside.

The Codex of Affixations on C

A page for everything you have known: each ability worn, each rune held and its Exquisite effect, each boss's gift once it has fallen, each stat with its cap and the pieces that roll it, each hazard met, and her claim. What it does, its numbers by quality or rank, where it comes from, and what you have of it now.

Levels of Atonement

Everything you do down there is experience, written at the lower left as it comes with what it was for: a kill by its kind, rank and level, a secret, a chain's end, a boss, a level cleared, a room first entered. The moment enough is banked for a level, a choir, a bell and a whisper sound and LEVEL OF ATONEMENT crosses the view. Only the merchant turns it into levels: her menu's ascend takes every one it reaches, ten points each, to the fiftieth, for the caster's own health, damage, resistances, spell haste, dodge, treasure, movement, Luck and attunement, added to what you wear under the same caps. Everyone starts alike, and a death takes it all.

The map 1,000 gold, once

A scroll floating at the merchant's left. M (up on a controller's D-pad) unrolls it across the view while the crypt waits: the level from above, north up, in brown ink on torn parchment, every wall within about three cells of you drawn as you pass, through the stone as well. Doors, the exit, keys you have seen, the Black Altar and opened secrets have their marks, and you are the red arrow. Move it about with W, A, S and D or the mouse; Space brings it back to you. It outlasts death.

The target's health

Look at a creature and its name, rank and health show at the top of the view, with the spell that hurts it most on the left and the one that hurts it least on the right, as far as you have learnt them by killing its kind: ? while that is still unknown, -- when no spell is better or worse than the rest.

Level n needs 42 × (n−1)² experience in all, 100,842 for the fiftieth; the screensaver's caster would reach it at about level 35 of the crypt, someone who clears every chain at about 29.

Cornerstones one at each tenth level

Free, one of five, chosen on a screen of their own. Second Wind: a blow that would kill you leaves you at half health instead, once a run of ten levels, or again for a Cursed Coin to the merchant. Premonition: a dashed purple edge on the floor a second before the place does harm there, and where an ember will land. Cartographer: each level's map comes already drawn, all but its secrets and keys. Lantern of the Dead: a pale light about you that shows the shades and keeps the Wraith two cells off. Grave Sense: creatures within ten cells on the map.

Overlevels past the fiftieth

At 55, 60 and every fifth level after, one line of your choice goes 5% past its cap, the cap with it (Luck 1%). An overlevelled number is drawn pale violet and breathing, with the dead's eye beside it, wherever it is shown.

Install

HAUNTED.EXE Setupv@VERSION@
  1. Download HAUNTED.EXE and double-click it to try it in a window. F11 switches to full screen and Esc quits.
  2. To make it your screensaver, right-click HAUNTED.scr and choose Install, or run install.cmd from the zip. Windows asks for administrator rights.
  3. In Screen Saver Settings choose HAUNTED.EXE. Settings… sets the sound, brightness, scenes, monitor and controller.
  4. To remove it, run uninstall.cmd as administrator.

Unsigned file: if SmartScreen stops it, choose More info, then Run anyway.

F1=Help Esc=Cancel

Upgrading from an earlier beta: your settings and saves carry over. This beta adds the map and your Levels of Atonement to each page of the ledger; the seventh and eighth betas step over that part, so they can still read its pages, without those. From the sixth, its graves open as pages of the ledger and its last progress as the unsigned run; sign it at the merchant's to keep it on a page. Saves from the seventh beta on can't be read by the sixth or earlier, so if you might go back that far, keep a copy first: reg export HKCU\Software\HalloweenVGASaver haunted-backup.reg. Double-click the file to restore it.

HAUNT.EXE's file manager shows your Windows user name and today's date. Keep that in mind before you share a recording. capture.cmd in the zip runs it as an ordinary window that OBS or Xbox Game Bar can record.

Built in the open

Every release is built from this repository by GitHub Actions. The MinGW-w64 cross-compiler builds the program, a headless harness renders both scenes and plays a scripted session, and the files go up with their SHA-256 checksums. It's about 30,000 lines of C with no dependencies beyond the standard Windows DLLs.

sudo apt install gcc-mingw-w64-x86-64 zlib1g-dev
make zip      # dist/HAUNTED.scr, dist/HAUNTED.EXE and the zip
make smoke    # both scenes and a scripted player in the test harness

Beta status

This is the thirteenth beta. The third was played by hand on the author's own Windows PC, on top of the automated tests, as were the ones before it. The fourth added things to wear rolled like loot, spirit and bones and the Black Altars; the fifth wisps that fly through the crypt and the status bar that steps aside; the sixth chains of secrets into the Backrooms and Severance and the Rune Shrine; the seventh, the biggest change the game has had, runs of ten levels with four bosses, death that costs, the merchant's ledger and her hall; the eighth a game controller; the ninth the map, the target's health, and Levels of Atonement with their cornerstones; the tenth a look of its own for each run of ten; the eleventh changed how the caster grows: spells as six elemental lines bought at the Lampstand, what the caster was, gear that rolls the elements, and souls that can run dry; the twelfth a new run from Esc, so that a person chooses what they were. This one gives each run hazards of its own, puzzles behind a sealed wing with their answers hidden on the level, trials against an hourglass, and a reckoning before you leave. Nothing from the fourth beta on has been played by hand yet, and the seventh to the thirteenth haven't been run under Wine either: they have been tested in the harness, and the Windows build compiled and smoke-tested by GitHub Actions.
