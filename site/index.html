WINDOWS SCREENSAVER · v@VERSION@ · BETA

HAUNTED.EXE

A Halloween screensaver for Windows that runs in real VGA modes. A possessed 486 boots, types back and falls apart; a Wolfenstein-style crypt crawl plays itself; and if you hold H for three seconds, the crypt is yours. It's made for OLED screens: true black, whole-number scaling up to 4K, and FM-synth music generated as it plays.

Two scenes

They take turns every few minutes, with a fade of the picture and the sound. You can turn either off in the settings.

HAUNT.EXE

80×25 text, 720×400

A possessed 486 PC. It boots, a ghost types back at the DOS prompt, and the programs have been got at: EDIT rewriting AUTOEXEC.BAT for a séance, DEFRAG moving souls to the front of the disk, a modem call to a BBS on the other side, MSD finding GHOST.SYS on IRQ 13. Between them come seven half-block ANSI scenes, from the jack-o'-lantern to a ghost that rushes down a hallway. It ends in a fatal error and a CRT power-off, then reboots.

CATACOMB

Mode 13h, 320×200, 256 colours

A Catacomb 3-D style crawl through crypts generated for each level: halls two and three cells wide, big chambers and colonnades, hung with chains, hooks, gibbet cages and chandeliers. Twenty named levels come in four sets of five, from the Crypts down to the Deep, each rotting further towards its end. The caster shops at the merchant, a cat on a treasure chest, for spells, runes and things to wear that are rolled like loot, then fights twenty kinds of creature with nine attack spells and nine runes, opening colour-coded vaults and hidden walls on the way to a rune-sealed exit. The creatures come in as you go down, in ranks you can tell by their colour, and they hunt you round corners. Will-o'-wisps, a soul each, fly ahead through the halls to the way out or a key and leave a trail of light to follow. From level 6, a Black Altar deep in each level sends its own wisp to the secrets, blesses your wisps against the dead, and anoints what you wear once the secrets are found.

The crypt is yours

Hold H for three seconds in either scene. A bar fills at the bottom of the screen; let go early and nothing happens. When it fills, the autopilot steps aside and the scenes stop switching until you hand it back. Any other key still ends the screensaver as usual.

Your defensive runes still fire on their own, as quickly and as fallibly as they do for the autopilot. The Wolfenstein status bar, with your spirit and, when the spear is in hand, your bones, slides away five seconds after anything it has to show, so you have the whole view; it comes back when you are hurt, choose a spell, tap H, or open the wardrobe or the bestiary, and stays up at the merchant's. Meanwhile a small readout shows gold, keys and souls as they come and go. Your hand rises only to cast or to reach for something, and sinks out of sight again.

What you wear. Seven places: a bracer on each wrist, boots, an amulet, a hood, robes and a ring. Every piece is rolled, and its quality shows in its colour: Common in white, Uncommon in green, Rare in blue, Ultra-Rare in purple, Exquisite in gold. The better the quality, the more stats and the stronger: armour, dodge, health, health restored, wards against poison, burning and slowing, movement, quiet footsteps, spell haste, spirit cost, damage, fire, frost or lightning, and spell effect. A hood always carries an ability, other pieces sometimes: Swift, Arcane, Treasure Hunter (a whisper near secret walls), Keymaker (keys shine through the stone), Communing (creatures as red shapes through the walls), Thorned and Mirrored. About one piece in ten comes Anointed, a little stronger. The robes colour your sleeve, the bracer shows as a cuff, the ring's stone takes its colour and the hood frames your face.

The merchant. Five to seven pedestals in an arc, deeper down more of them, and one to three purchases a visit. A piece is worn the moment you buy it. You start without a bag; bags hold two to five spare pieces, and without one, what a new piece replaces is lost, so the card warns you first in red. With a full bag you are asked which spare to let go.

Spirit and bones. Your souls are your spirit, and spells spend it, to the tenth. The Fire Bolt you start with, and any spell at its tier-I strength, costs nothing; a stronger cast costs a tenth of a soul to a whole one, and with too little spirit a spell goes out weak. Every Bone Spear throws a bone, which skeletons, shield-bearers, knights and hounds drop now and then.

Secrets. Every level hides one to three walls that give: one on the first two levels, two from level 3 and three from level 6. They look like plain stone until you are a step or two away, when cracks show in them. Press E and the wall grinds back into a hidden closet. Inside is treasure and often a potion, never a key, and none of it counts towards the level's treasure, so whatever you find is extra. The tally at the end of the level counts the secrets you found.

Wisps. Press L and a wisp to the way out costs a soul. It kindles before you and flies off down the halls the way you would walk, fetching the key first if the exit is in a locked vault, and waits at the doorstep. It lights the dark as it goes, shows the shades and drives the Wraith back, and leaves motes of light hanging for twelve seconds, so you can follow it round corners or after a fight. With a Keymaker piece on, there is a wisp for each key still lying in the level, in that key's colour.

Black Altars. From level 6, an altar stands deep in each level that has secrets. Press E at it for its three gifts, each with its price and a note on why it can't be had yet. Ten spirit wakes its wisp: a violet flame lights in its bowl, and a violet wisp leads you, free, to every secret wall on the level, through any vault key on the way. Twenty-five blesses your exit wisps until you leave the level: they wear a pale gold halo, and the undead near them flee. Find every secret and the flame burns gold; then it anoints one thing you wear that isn't anointed already. The autopilot uses the altars too, and casts a wisp when it heads for the way out.

Secrets were added.

Who lives down there. About one new kind of creature a level, each a level or two after its sign, and alone the first time. In the Crypts, skeletons, bats and spectres, shamblers climbing out of turned graves, skulls in the walls and shield-bearers. In the Catacombs, spiders in their webs, the Drowned rising from puddles, bone hounds in pairs, and the Wraith, once a level, which nothing kills: fire and a wisp's light only drive it back. In the Hollows, hunting spiders on the ceiling, ghouls that feed on the dead, skeleton knights whose plate comes off as you hurt them, and brood mothers with their egg sacs. In the Deep, shades you see only in light, which take your souls, ember imps by the magma, deacons that raise the dead (kill them first), and gargoyles that are statues until you pass.

Ranks. Each creature comes in its own colour or in green, blue, red or black, each tougher, quicker and worth more souls than the last, and the deeper you go, the higher the mix. Now and then one is gilded: it runs when hurt, and carries treasure.

Achievements and the bestiary. Each run keeps a record of what it has killed, the secrets it has found and the levels it has cleared, saved with the run and with each grave. Achievements come for 60 of a creature's lowest rank and fewer for each rank up, for secrets from the first to the fiftieth, and for every level to 20 and each time down after; they slide in at the upper right. Press B for the bestiary: a page for each creature, turning on the spot in each rank you have met, with your kills and what hurts it, learnt from three kills with each spell. The record is kept when you flee or carve a grave; what the autopilot earns in an ordinary screensaver session isn't.

LEAVE THE CRYPT?

THE CAT IS WATCHING. THE CAT IS ALWAYS WATCHING.

BACK TO THE CRYPT

  • Flee to the surface ends the screensaver and saves your level, health, gold, souls and gear. Next time you start at the beginning of that level, and the next session starts from it whether you play or not.
  • Stay in the dark closes the question. It's the default, so a stray Enter keeps you playing.
  • Sleepwalk hands the crypt back to the autopilot, which walks on from where you stood.
  • Carve a gravestone saves the run in one of three graves. Carving over a used grave asks twice.
  • Rise from a grave loads one, from the beginning of its level, and the controls stay yours.

Install

HAUNTED.EXE Setupv@VERSION@
  1. Download HAUNTED.EXE and double-click it to try it in a window. F11 switches to full screen and Esc quits.
  2. To make it your screensaver, right-click HAUNTED.scr and choose Install, or run install.cmd from the zip. Windows asks for administrator rights.
  3. In Screen Saver Settings choose HAUNTED.EXE. Settings… sets the sound, brightness, scenes and monitor.
  4. To remove it, run uninstall.cmd as administrator.

Unsigned file: if SmartScreen stops it, choose More info, then Run anyway.

F1=Help Esc=Cancel

Upgrading from an earlier beta: your settings, progress and graves carry over (from before the fourth beta, with your old robe, hood and best ring turned into Common pieces). This beta adds a little to the save, which earlier betas can't read, so if you might go back, keep a copy first: reg export HKCU\Software\HalloweenVGASaver haunted-backup.reg. Double-click the file to restore it.

HAUNT.EXE's file manager shows your Windows user name and today's date. Keep that in mind before you share a recording. capture.cmd in the zip runs it as an ordinary window that OBS or Xbox Game Bar can record.

Built in the open

Every release is built from this repository by GitHub Actions. The MinGW-w64 cross-compiler builds the program, a headless harness renders both scenes and plays a scripted session, and the files go up with their SHA-256 checksums. It's about 22,000 lines of C with no dependencies beyond the standard Windows DLLs.

sudo apt install gcc-mingw-w64-x86-64 zlib1g-dev
make zip      # dist/HAUNTED.scr, dist/HAUNTED.EXE and the zip
make smoke    # both scenes and a scripted player in the test harness
